# Lambda to build new data center in Mayes County, Oklahoma, generating half a billion dollars in tax revenue over next decade

 September 27, 2026

 • 7 min read

Facility will be built in MidAmerica Industrial Park & deploy closed-loop cooling technology to preserve and recycle water; all energy costs paid in compliance with Oklahoma’s ratepayer protections

CHOUTEAU, OK, September 27, 2026 — Lambda Inc. (“Lambda”), the Superintelligence Cloud, today announced plans to build a new data center at MidAmerica Industrial Park (MidAmerica) in Mayes County, Oklahoma.

Lambda is expected to pay an estimated $500 million in taxes over the next 10 years based on current rates, and the project is expected to create up to 1,000 construction jobs and up to 100 permanent positions. 

In compliance with Oklahoma’s Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act, Lambda will take full responsibility for the resources and infrastructure needed to operate the facility, including paying 100 percent of its energy costs. The company will also manage local resources responsibly by minimizing water use through closed-loop cooling technology and optimizing energy efficiency.

“Lambda is committed to being a good neighbor and long-term partner with the town of Chouteau and Mayes County,” said Stephen Balaban, Co-Founder and Chief Technology Officer at Lambda. “We’ve been meeting with local leaders to understand their priorities and help shape our long-term investments in the community. We're going to keep listening, keep showing up, and let our work here speak for itself."

“When developed responsibly, data centers can create lasting economic opportunities and investments in the communities where they operate,” said Michel Combes, Chief Executive Officer of Lambda. “Our responsibility is to ensure that this project delivers value to Mayes County while respecting the community’s resources, infrastructure, and priorities. We are committed to meeting that responsibility at every stage of the project.”

## Lambda’s commitments

- Ratepayer protection: Lambda will protect local ratepayers by paying for all electricity the facility uses and ensuring the project meets Oklahoma’s requirements under the state’s ratepayer protection laws. The company has also signed the White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge and is working to design the project to be a more flexible grid customer with the ability to shift or reduce certain computing loads during periods of peak demand.

- Water stewardship: The facility will implement closed-loop cooling technology that dramatically reduces water use compared with traditional data center cooling.

- Siting away from residents: The project is being built at the existing MidAmerica Industrial Park with infrastructure designed to minimize impacts on surrounding residential areas. 

"Oklahoma is leading the nation in building the modern economy, bringing major investments to our state and creating opportunities that will benefit our people for generations to come,” said Governor Kevin Stitt. “Lambda has committed to taking responsibility for the energy costs associated with its facility, ensuring those costs are not passed along to residential customers while generating hundreds of millions in tax revenue and economic growth right here in Oklahoma. These are commitments that reflect the kind of responsible investment we want to see in Oklahoma.”

“Major investments like this demonstrate the opportunity we have to continue growing Oklahoma’s economy and attracting innovative companies to our state,” said Oklahoma Department of Commerce CEO John Budd. “Lambda's decision to build here is a vote of confidence in Oklahoma’s workforce, infrastructure and business-friendly environment. We look forward to supporting this project and the new opportunities it will create for Oklahomans, while continuing to build an economy that can compete for investment and high-quality jobs for years to come.”

“Lambda’s decision to build in Mayes County represents a transformative commitment to economic development in our community,” said Chouteau Mayor Brenda Cunningham. “This project will bring an estimated $500 million in tax revenue to our county over the next decade, which will go toward supporting our schools, public services and local priorities. This is the kind of partnership we want in our community."

“This is a significant investment in Mayes County and an important opportunity for our community,” said Steve Grossman, Mayes County Commissioner. “Beyond the economic activity it will bring, this project will strengthen our local tax base and create new resources that can support schools, infrastructure and other community priorities for years to come. We’re pleased to welcome Lambda to Mayes County and look forward to working with them as the project moves forward.”

The new data center will be located in the south section of the industrial park and will sit within the Chouteau-Mazie Public School District tax boundary.

“This investment will have a lasting impact on Chouteau-Mazie District Schools and the students we serve,” said Josh Gwartney, Chouteau-Mazie Public School Superintendent. “The tax revenue will support our schools and allow us to invest in better facilities, competitive teacher salaries, advanced programs and more career and technical education opportunities. We’re excited about the opportunities this creates for our students and teachers.”

Additionally, MidAmerica selected Lambda from a range of potential partners based on the company’s approach to responsible development and building standards.

“MidAmerica and GRDA took a deliberate approach to finding the right partner. Through what we call a reverse RFP, we started with the strengths of this site and looked for a developer whose plans would deliver lasting value to the region. Lambda stood out as a major investor, a responsible developer and operator, a good steward of resources and a strong community partner. Lambda’s commitment to investing in education and quality of life initiatives begins now, while the project is still under development,” said David Stewart, CEO and member, Board of Trustees of MidAmerica Industrial Park. "As construction gets underway, the project is expected to create approximately 1,000 jobs and generate opportunities for local vendors, contractors and suppliers. That means the community will begin to see a positive economic impact as the project takes shape. We are proud we found a partner that will make a positive impact from the start and build on it for years to come.”

“Grand River Dam Authority (GRDA) created a focused, comprehensive planning process when evaluating potential projects of this magnitude. GRDA has made the prudent and responsible investments necessary for the past decade to ensure we are ready for projects of this scale and impact,” said Dan Sullivan, CEO, Grand River Dam Authority. “Lambda will fund the upfront infrastructure costs required to serve the project, ensuring those expenses are not passed on to other GRDA customers. That approach, combined with the project's positive economic impact, responsible, and proactive approach to this investment, along with the tax benefits, is why we believe this development delivers a strong return for all those we serve.”

Cloverleaf Infrastructure was selected by GRDA and MidAmerica to ready the Mayes County site for development by Lambda. 

"Cloverleaf is proud that Grand River Dam Authority and MidAmerica Industrial Park selected us to help ready this site for future development. As a public power leader and an economic development champion, respectively, both have been strong advocates for the Mayes County community, and we have greatly valued our partnership with them,” said Aaron Bilyeu, Chief Development Officer at Cloverleaf Infrastructure. “As we move forward, we will be guided by the Cloverleaf Standard, our commitment to developing critical infrastructure responsibly, transparently and in partnership with the communities we serve. We are confident in Lambda’s ability to lead the project, and expect it to create significant construction jobs, generate long-term employment opportunities, and deliver meaningful tax revenue that will support local priorities, public services, and infrastructure investments for years to come.”

In the coming weeks, Lambda and MidAmerica will continue meeting with local leaders and residents to listen and gather input to help guide community investment. As planning progresses, more information will be shared about the project timeline and its expected economic impact.

## About MidAmerica Industrial Park

MidAmerica Industrial Park is the 8th largest industrial park in the world. Situated on 9,000 acres, MidAmerica is located in Northeast Oklahoma and centered between Tulsa, Okla., and Bentonville, Ark. MidAmerica is within 300 miles of 10 major metropolitan cities and has a next-day service capability to 23% of the U.S. population. Its transportation distribution and logistics boasts access to four interstates, on-site Union Pacific rail and switchyard, the nation’s most inland water port, and two international airports. More than 80 firms are located within MidAmerica, including the Nation’s largest Google Data Center and five Fortune 500 companies. MidAmerica is operated by a public trust and offers unparalleled speed-to-market opportunities for business expansions. For more information, visit MAIP.com.

## About Grand River Dam Authority

Grand River Dam Authority (GRDA), Oklahoma’s largest public power electric utility, is fully funded through electric and water sales rather than taxes. Guided by its commitment to the 5 E’s—electricity, economic development, environmental stewardship, employees, and efficiency—GRDA continues to strive as an “Oklahoma agency of excellence.” For more information, visit GRDA.com.

## About Cloverleaf Infrastructure

Cloverleaf Infrastructure is a real estate developer that partners with investors, energy innovators and utilities to deliver high-quality, clean-powered shovel-ready sites for technology companies that build and operate data centers, the facilities that power the internet, cloud services and everyday digital tools. Cloverleaf was formed in 2024 with an initial investment from Sandbrook Capital and NGP Energy Capital. In 2026, Cloverleaf entered into a strategic partnership with NVIDIA to further advance digital infrastructure development across the United States.

Cloverleaf believes essential infrastructure should deliver lasting value. Its projects create jobs, generate tax revenue, strengthen regional energy resilience and support long-term economic growth while carefully managing impacts on local infrastructure, natural resources and the surrounding landscape.
