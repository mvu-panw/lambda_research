# VOICES OFAI Research
A survey of Lambda Research Grant recipients on the state of their fields, the compute bottleneck, and what comes next.

## Introduction

The ideas that define AI start in labs, with a researcher, a hypothesis, a dataset, and a cluster. The papers published at ICML, NeurIPS, and CVPR today become the foundation for what frontier labs ship next.

This report is built from the responses of more than 200 researchers in the Lambda Research Grant Program. We asked them what they're working on, what progress matters most, what stands in their way, and where they think their fields are headed.

Lambda's Research Grant Program exists to remove the bottleneck between research and compute. Lambda has distributed over $1.3M in cloud credits through the program, and that compute has powered over 150 published papers. We provide compute to researchers so they can spend their time on experiments.

## The work

We asked researchers about their current projects, what they set out to do, what came of it, and how they used the compute.

Most projects run on short timelines and small datasets. That fits what researchers told us about compute: long, large-scale runs need more than most labs can get.

## What gets in the way

The researchers were asked to stack-rank a handful of common problems: data quality, researcher headcount, meeting publication deadlines, and more. Across all areas of research, one answer topped all others.

The biggest problem for researchers is their lack of compute. It keeps novel AI research ideas from becoming findings and validated claims. Labs try to close the gap through funding cycles, but funding reviews take months, while architectures and baselines shift in weeks. By the time compute is approved, the proposed experiment is outdated. So researchers spend their time writing proposals instead of running the experiments they've trained for years to run.

That gap, between the work researchers want to do and the infrastructure they can reach, is shaping where the field goes next.

The mismatch between grant timelines and research timelines shows up in how researchers describe the process itself. When we asked what frustrates them most about funding applications, compute and long, complex processes came up again and again. Every week spent on paperwork is a week not spent on experiments.

“Compute lets me scale dataset size, run many parallel simulation environments for faster-than-real-time rollout and MPC-guided fine-tuning, and sweep conditioning and consistency designs in days instead of weeks, which is the difference between testing one idea and finding the one that works.”
Deemo Chen, Caltech

“Compute converts almost linearly into statistical power and into the ability to validate claims at a scale where they actually transfer.”
Tianyi Zhang, University of Washington
