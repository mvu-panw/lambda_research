The 30-second version

  • Shape of the build: Lambda is a North American builder. Its infrastructure pages map data centers across "the United States and Canada", and its VP of Data Center Infrastructure has said the company is "focused right now in the US because it really is ground zero". The large non-US build is Quebec, and a second North American site is being fitted out in Querétaro, Mexico. Confirmed infrastructure podcast
  • Reach beyond North America is lighter: the On-Demand docs list five non-US regions (Tokyo, Osaka, India, Germany, Israel), and Lambda bills customers in 20 countries and regions. No Lambda-run facility, local hiring, or press release backs any of the five, so they are most likely small colocation or partner deployments. Confirmed regions exist regions Inferred scale
  • Positioning: Lambda markets "distributed AI factories" of 5–75 MW in strategic metros to "meet sovereign-AI and data-protection laws", alongside 75 MW+ contiguous campuses. That is the hook for in-country AI capacity, but Lambda has announced no sovereign deal outside the US. Confirmed infrastructure
  • Why it matters for PANW: each country adds an edge, management and OOB footprint under different data-residency rules, run by a security team based entirely in the US. One policy plane across Quebec, Mexico and the overseas regions is the pitch. Inferred details ↓

What it means for PANW Inferred

  • Quebec is the near-term target. It is Lambda's largest disclosed non-US fabric, it is live, and Lambda's own staff are turning up carrier DIA circuits there. It needs the same north-south design as the US Private Cloud: PA-5580 HA at the DIA edge, plus a management and OOB tier. Ask whether it also uses FortiGate.
  • Data-residency rules differ in every country. Quebec's Law 25, Mexico's LFPDPPP, the GDPR and the EU AI Act in Germany, Israel's privacy law, and India's DPDP Act all apply. Customers buying there will ask for in-country logging and isolation evidence. Strata Cloud Manager or Panorama gives one policy and logging plane with per-region log residency, which supports "distributed AI factories" for sovereign buyers.
  • A US-only security team runs a multi-country estate. Every security role is in the Bay Area or Bellevue, and Querétaro and Quebec are staffed by DC technicians only. Remote, centrally managed firewalls with zero-touch provisioning and XSIAM-run SOC automation suit sites with no local security staff.
  • Backbone and interconnect. A dedicated optical backbone and customer-interconnect org (Dublin lead) means inter-site and customer links are being designed now. Position encrypted, high-throughput DCI at the backbone edge and Prisma Access for staff working across countries. Firewalls stay out of the GPU fabric.
  • Mexico is the next greenfield. The colo fit-out is happening now, so the network design, including "network installers" in the fit-out scope, is being set. Ask who owns the edge standard and whether Querétaro serves US customers, nearshoring demand, or LATAM.
  • Who to find: the Senior Director of Capacity Delivery (the Dublin role reports there), the Staff Security Architect (who sets standards across sites), and VP of Data Center Infrastructure Ken Patchett.
