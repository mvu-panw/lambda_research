Architecture

Lambda's two published topologies, redrawn from their security-posture docs, with where PANW enforcement points land.

Key point: Lambda's security-posture pages name no firewall vendor, model, or link speed. Every firewall sits north-south (internet edge, management, out-of-band). The InfiniBand/RoCE GPU fabric is never firewalled, and the posture should stay that way.

Lambda Private Cloud

Open full PDF Lambda security posture doc

What Lambda documents

  • Single-tenant, end to end: "All network connections and supporting infrastructure (including firewalls, routers, and switches) are dedicated to the customer's cluster."
  • Edge: redundant dedicated internet access (DIA) links into an HA perimeter firewall. No internet-exposed ingress ports by default, and the customer controls policy.
  • VPN: IPsec site-to-site tunnels and client VPN terminate on the perimeter firewall.
  • Fabrics: in-band Ethernet plus an InfiniBand spine-leaf fabric for GPU RDMA. RoCE may replace IB depending on cluster size. "All compute nodes have unrestricted access to each other on this fabric."
  • Management plane: BMCs, DPUs, and switch management sit on a separate management network that reaches in-band only through a dedicated management firewall.
  • Out-of-band: its own OOB firewall on a low-bandwidth backup (cellular) link. "General routing is not permitted over the OOB backup link."
  • Crypto: AES-256-XTS at rest with a key per cluster. Intra-cluster traffic is not encrypted.

PANW enforcement points

  1. Perimeter HA pair on the dual DIA links. This is where performance sizing matters. PA-5500 / PA-7500 →
  2. Management firewall between mgmt and in-band. Low throughput, high value: it protects BMC/DPU/switch management from a compromised tenant workload.
  3. OOB firewall with built-in 5G for the cellular backup path.
  4. Optional host edge: Prisma AIRS on BlueField-3 for low-rate inference/API ingress per node, not the training path.

Talk track: Lambda already has three firewall tiers by design. The question is whose firewalls they are, and whether the edge tier inspects at line rate with full threat prevention turned on.

Lambda 1-Click Clusters

Open full PDF Lambda security posture doc

What Lambda documents

  • Head (management) nodes: multi-tenant hardware, isolated by hardware virtualization, with NICs shared with other customers. Directly reachable over the internet via SSH by default. Customers write their own inbound rules.
  • Compute (GPU) nodes: single-tenant, on a dedicated segment with no inbound connectivity from the firewall. Tenant isolation is logical segmentation.
  • Ethernet: a logically isolated fabric shared by compute and head nodes.
  • InfiniBand: customer traffic "only ever transits physical IB links dedicated to that customer."
  • Storage: an isolated network that only head and compute nodes can reach. Encrypted at rest; algorithm not stated.
  • Internet: via an internet gateway and NAT gateway behind HA perimeter firewalls on two DIA links, which are shared across tenants (2×100G at the 2024 H100 launch).

PANW enforcement points

  1. Lambda's shared cloud edge: the perimeter that carries traffic for every 1CC tenant. Aggregate bandwidth grows with the fleet, so this is the PA-7500 conversation.
  2. Tenant ingress to head nodes: SSH and JupyterLab are internet-exposed by default on shared hardware. That is the most visible risk in either topology, and a natural place for per-tenant policy (Prisma AIRS on the DPU, or vsys/zones at the edge).
  3. Head-to-compute boundary: logical segmentation only. Inspection here is low-rate control traffic, not RDMA.

Talk track: 1CC is a multi-tenant service. Lambda's own platform security, including the shared edge, head-node exposure, and tenant isolation evidence for enterprise buyers, is a different sale from the per-customer firewalls in Private Cloud.
